Technical SEO Audit Checklist: 25 Steps to Fix Your Site Architecture

AR
ARISE Editorial Team
April 1, 2025
18 min read
Traffic & Acquisition
🔎

Technical SEO is the invisible infrastructure that determines whether Google can find, crawl, understand, and rank your content. You can publish the most insightful articles in your industry, build thousands of backlinks, and nail your on-page optimization — but if your site has critical technical issues, you'll never reach your organic traffic potential.

This checklist consolidates 25 actionable steps organized into five categories: Crawlability, Indexation, Performance, Site Architecture, and Structured Data. Work through each section systematically, and you'll emerge with a technically sound site ready to compete at the highest level.

Why Technical SEO Matters More Than Ever

Google's algorithms have become dramatically more sophisticated in evaluating technical quality. Core Web Vitals are now confirmed ranking signals. Mobile-first indexing means your mobile experience is your primary experience. And with the rise of AI-powered search features, structured data is becoming a direct pathway to visibility in AI-generated answers.

A study by SEMrush found that 65% of analyzed websites had critical crawlability issues. Screaming Frog data suggests that broken internal links affect 42% of crawled sites. These aren't edge cases — they're widespread problems affecting real rankings every day.

Section 1: Crawlability (Steps 1–5)

Step 1: Audit Your robots.txt File

Navigate to yourdomain.com/robots.txt. Verify that you're not accidentally blocking important directories (like /blog/, /products/, or your CSS/JS files). Use Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool to test specific pages if you suspect crawl blocks.

Common mistake: Disallowing Disallow: / on staging sites and forgetting to update before launch.

Step 2: Find and Fix Crawl Traps

Crawl traps are URLs that generate infinite variations: calendar archives, session IDs in URLs, infinite scroll parameters, and faceted navigation. Use Screaming Frog or Sitebulb to identify URL patterns with parameters and determine whether to noindex, canonicalize, or block them.

Step 3: Resolve All 4xx and 5xx Server Errors

Export your site's crawl data and filter for 404 (not found) and 5xx (server error) responses. For 404 pages with significant backlinks, implement 301 redirects. For 404 pages with no value, remove internal links pointing to them. Recurring 5xx errors require server-level investigation.

Step 4: Analyze Crawl Budget Efficiency

For sites with thousands of pages, crawl budget is finite. Use Google Search Console's Crawl Stats report (Settings → Crawl Stats) to see how Googlebot is spending its crawl budget. If it's wasting time on low-value URLs (tag pages, archive pages, parameter pages), add noindex or robots.txt disallow directives.

Step 5: Check Internal Link Depth

No important page should be more than 3 clicks from the homepage. Use Screaming Frog's "Crawl Depth" report to identify pages buried deep in your site hierarchy. Create topic hub pages or add cross-linking between related content to bring orphaned pages within reach.

Section 2: Indexation (Steps 6–10)

Step 6: Verify Indexation Status

Use the site: search operator (e.g., site:yourdomain.com) and compare the result count against your actual page count. Large discrepancies indicate indexation problems. Cross-reference with Google Search Console's Coverage report for detailed status breakdowns.

Step 7: Audit Noindex Tags

Search your CMS and HTML for <meta name="robots" content="noindex">. Verify that only pages you want excluded are tagged: paginated internal search results, duplicate content pages, thin content, and staging/preview URLs. Any valuable page with a noindex tag is invisible to Google.

Step 8: Fix Canonical Tag Issues

Canonical tags tell Google which version of a page to index when duplicates exist. Common issues include: self-referencing canonicals that point to the wrong URL, missing canonicals on paginated series, and canonical chains (A points to B, B points to C). Use Screaming Frog to audit all canonical implementations.

Step 9: Audit Your XML Sitemap

Your XML sitemap should include only URLs you want indexed: no noindex pages, no 301-redirected URLs, no 404 pages. Check that your sitemap is submitted in Google Search Console and that Google reports no errors. For large sites, use a sitemap index file with separate sitemaps for different content types.

Step 10: Address Thin and Duplicate Content

Pages with fewer than 300 words of unique, substantive content are candidates for consolidation, expansion, or noindex. Run a site-wide content audit using Screaming Frog to identify pages by word count and use Copyscape or Siteliner to flag internal duplicate content.

Section 3: Performance / Core Web Vitals (Steps 11–15)

Step 11: Measure Your Core Web Vitals

Google's Core Web Vitals — Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Interaction to Next Paint (INP), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) — are direct ranking signals. Measure them in Google Search Console (Core Web Vitals report) and PageSpeed Insights for field data, and use Lighthouse for lab data.

Targets: LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1.

Step 12: Optimize Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)

LCP measures how fast your largest visible element loads. The most common culprits: unoptimized hero images, render-blocking CSS and fonts, slow server response times (TTFB). Solutions: convert images to WebP, implement lazy loading, preload critical fonts, use a CDN.

Step 13: Eliminate Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)

CLS measures visual stability — how much the page jumps as it loads. Common causes: images without defined dimensions, late-loading ads, dynamically injected content above existing content. Fix by always declaring width and height on all images and iframes.

Step 14: Implement Image Optimization

Images account for 50–60% of page weight on most websites. Optimize by: converting to WebP or AVIF format (30–50% smaller than JPEG), implementing responsive images with srcset, adding the loading="lazy" attribute to all below-the-fold images, and using a CDN for faster delivery.

Step 15: Minimize Render-Blocking Resources

CSS and JavaScript that load synchronously in the document <head> block page rendering. Audit using Chrome DevTools' Performance tab. Move non-critical CSS to inline or defer it. Add defer or async attributes to non-critical JavaScript.

Section 4: Site Architecture (Steps 16–20)

Step 16: Enforce HTTPS Everywhere

Verify that all pages load over HTTPS (including all assets — images, fonts, scripts). Mixed content warnings (HTTPS page loading HTTP resources) can cause browser warnings and indexation issues. Check for HTTP-to-HTTPS redirect chains and canonicalization mismatches.

Step 17: Implement Logical URL Structure

URLs should be short, descriptive, and keyword-rich. Avoid numbers, dates, and auto-generated strings in URLs. Use hyphens (not underscores) to separate words. Maintain consistent trailing slash usage. Avoid URL parameters for navigational content.

Step 18: Audit Internal Linking Strategy

Internal links distribute "link equity" (PageRank) throughout your site. Ensure your most important pages receive the most internal links from high-authority pages. Use descriptive anchor text (not "click here" or "read more"). Run a Screaming Frog crawl and sort by inbound internal links to identify which pages are getting the most signals.

Step 19: Fix Redirect Chains and Loops

Each redirect in a chain loses some link equity and slows down the crawl. Identify redirect chains (A→B→C) and loops (A→B→A) using Screaming Frog and resolve them to single, direct redirects. All 302 temporary redirects that have been in place for 6+ months should be converted to 301 permanent redirects.

Step 20: Verify Mobile-First Implementation

Since 2019, Google uses mobile-first indexing for all new sites — meaning the mobile version of your site is what Google indexes and ranks. Test every page type with Google's Mobile-Friendly Test. Ensure content, structured data, and metadata are identical (or better) on mobile compared to desktop.

Section 5: Structured Data (Steps 21–25)

Step 21: Implement Schema.org Markup

Structured data in JSON-LD format helps Google understand your content and qualify it for rich results (star ratings, FAQs, how-to steps, breadcrumbs, sitelinks searchbox). Implement at minimum: Organization, WebSite (with SearchAction), and the appropriate content type (BlogPosting, Product, FAQPage) for each page type.

Step 22: Validate All Structured Data

Use Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org Validator to validate every structured data implementation. Fix all errors (which prevent rich results) and address warnings (which reduce eligibility). Monitor Google Search Console's Rich Results report for ongoing validation issues.

Step 23: Implement Breadcrumb Markup

BreadcrumbList schema tells Google your site hierarchy and enables breadcrumb display in search results. Breadcrumbs in SERPs improve CTR by making results more navigable. Implement on all internal pages and validate using the Rich Results Test.

Step 24: Add FAQ and How-To Schema Where Appropriate

FAQPage schema on pages that answer common questions can unlock FAQ rich results, doubling your SERP real estate. HowTo schema on instructional content can display step-by-step rich results. Both are high-value opportunities that most sites are not yet exploiting.

Step 25: Implement Hreflang for International Sites

If your site targets multiple languages or regions, implement hreflang tags to tell Google which version to serve to which audience. Missing or incorrect hreflang causes Google to serve the wrong language version to users, increasing bounce rates and reducing rankings in international markets.

Recommended Tools for This Audit

  • Screaming Frog SEO Spider: The go-to desktop crawler for comprehensive technical audits
  • Sitebulb: Excellent visualization of site architecture and crawl data
  • Google Search Console: Essential for real Googlebot data (Coverage, Core Web Vitals, Rich Results)
  • PageSpeed Insights / Lighthouse: Performance measurement against Core Web Vitals thresholds
  • SEMrush Site Audit: Cloud-based ongoing technical monitoring
  • Ahrefs Site Audit: Strong broken link and redirect chain detection
  • Schema.org Validator: Structured data validation

Prioritizing Your Technical SEO Fixes

After completing all 25 steps, you'll likely have a list of issues. Prioritize them using this simple matrix:

  1. Critical & High-Impact: Fix immediately. Crawl blocks, widespread noindex errors, major Core Web Vitals failures.
  2. Important but Contained: Address in the next sprint. Redirect chains, missing structured data, thin content.
  3. Low-Hanging Fruit: Quick wins. Image optimization, canonical cleanup, internal linking improvements.
  4. Long-Term Projects: Site architecture overhauls, international SEO implementation.

Technical SEO is not a one-time project — it's a continuous discipline. Re-run this audit every six months or after any major site change (CMS migration, redesign, URL structure change).
