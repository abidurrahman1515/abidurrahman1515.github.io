How to Write High-Converting Landing Page Copy (With Framework)

Your landing page has one job. A single, clear, measurable job: convert the right visitor into a lead, trial user, or customer. And yet the average landing page conversion rate is just 2.35%. The top 10% of pages achieve over 11% conversion rates (Wordstream). The difference is almost entirely in the copy.

The Big Three Copy Frameworks

PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve

Identify the reader's Problem. Twist the knife by Agitating it — expanding on the consequences of not solving it. Then position your offer as the Solution. PAS is most effective for pain-point-driven products where the reader is already aware of their problem.

AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

The classic framework. Attention: a headline that disrupts the pattern. Interest: subheadline that deepens curiosity. Desire: build emotional and logical appetite through benefits, not features. Action: a clear, single CTA that removes friction from conversion.

BAB — Before, After, Bridge

Paint the picture of life Before your solution — current pain. Paint the picture of life After — the aspirational outcome. Then present your offer as the Bridge between the two states. BAB works especially well for transformation-oriented products (fitness, education, business tools).

Copy Principle: Sell the destination, not the vehicle. Customers don't want a mattress — they want a great night's sleep. Lead with outcomes, support with features.

The Headline: Your Most Important Investment

David Ogilvy famously said: "On average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy." A compelling headline must do four things: be immediately clear about what the page offers, speak directly to the target persona, convey the primary benefit or outcome, and create enough curiosity or urgency to pull the visitor into the next line.

The fastest way to improve headline performance is to test specificity. "Get More Leads" will always underperform "Get 47% More Qualified Leads in 30 Days."

CTA Optimization

The CTA button is the final conversion gate. Test these elements:

  • Specificity: "Start Free Trial" outperforms "Submit" — always use action-oriented, specific verbs.
  • First-person phrasing: "Start My Free Trial" outperforms "Start Your Free Trial" by up to 90% (Michael Aagaard research).
  • Value reinforcement: Add a micro-copy line beneath the button: "No credit card required. Cancel anytime."
  • Contrast: Your CTA button must be visually dominant on the page. Do not let it blend into the design.

Social Proof Placement

Social proof should appear at every friction point on the page. Place logo bars immediately below the hero section (borrowed authority). Include a testimonial directly alongside your primary CTA (reduces hesitation). Quantify everything: "4.9 stars across 2,340 reviews" beats "Great reviews!"

Page Structure for High Conversion

  1. Hero section: Headline, subheadline, primary CTA, and one trust signal
  2. Social proof bar: Logos of recognizable clients or media features
  3. Benefits section: 3–5 key outcomes (not features) with brief explanations
  4. Proof section: Case study or detailed testimonial with specific results
  5. Objection handling: FAQ or "How it works" section addressing the top 3 hesitations
  6. Final CTA: Repeat the primary offer with reinforced social proof

Key Takeaways

  • Match your copy framework (PAS, AIDA, BAB) to your customer's awareness level.
  • Your headline is your single highest-leverage copywriting element — invest disproportionate effort.
  • First-person CTA phrasing consistently outperforms second-person in A/B tests.
  • Place social proof at every friction point on the page, not just at the bottom.
  • Message match between your ad and landing page is a quick win for conversion rate.
