LTV:CAC Ratio: The Ultimate Metric for Sustainable Business Growth

📅 April 1, 2026 🕑 7 min read ✎ ARISE Editorial Team

In This Article

  1. What Is LTV:CAC?
  2. Calculating LTV
  3. Calculating CAC
  4. Industry Benchmarks
  5. How to Improve Your Ratio
  6. Key Takeaways

If you could only track one metric to determine the long-term health of your business, the LTV:CAC ratio would be it. It answers a single, critical question: for every dollar you spend acquiring a customer, how many dollars do you get back over the lifetime of that relationship?

A ratio below 1:1 means you are losing money on every customer you acquire. A ratio of 3:1 is the widely-cited benchmark for a healthy SaaS business. A ratio above 5:1 suggests you may be underinvesting in growth.

What Is the LTV:CAC Ratio?

Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) is the total revenue a business can expect from a single customer account throughout their relationship. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is the total cost of convincing a prospective customer to purchase, including marketing and sales costs.

The ratio is calculated as: LTV ÷ CAC. A ratio of 3 means for every $1 spent acquiring a customer, you earn $3 back over their lifetime.

Rule of Thumb: A healthy SaaS business targets an LTV:CAC ratio of 3:1. Below 1:1 is existential; above 5:1 suggests you should be spending more on growth.

Calculating Customer Lifetime Value

The most common formula for LTV in a subscription business is:

LTV = ARPU × Gross Margin % ÷ Churn Rate

For example: If your Average Revenue Per User is $50/month, your gross margin is 70%, and your monthly churn rate is 2.5%, then:

LTV = $50 × 0.70 ÷ 0.025 = $1,400

This means you can theoretically afford to spend up to $1,400 to acquire a customer and break even — though you would want to spend significantly less to maintain profitability.

Calculating Customer Acquisition Cost

The formula is: CAC = (Total Sales & Marketing Costs) ÷ (Number of New Customers Acquired)

A critical mistake is to include only paid advertising in your CAC calculation. You must include all sales and marketing expenses: salaries, agency fees, software tools, event costs, and content production. Under-counting CAC is one of the most common ways startups misrepresent their unit economics.

Industry Benchmarks

  • Exceptional: LTV:CAC > 5:1 (but may indicate underinvestment in growth)
  • Healthy: LTV:CAC = 3:1 to 5:1
  • Borderline: LTV:CAC = 1:1 to 3:1
  • Unsustainable: LTV:CAC < 1:1

The CAC Payback Period — the time it takes to recover your CAC — should be under 12 months for most SaaS businesses. For enterprise SaaS, up to 18 months is acceptable if the contracts are long-term and secure.

How to Improve Your LTV:CAC Ratio

There are two levers: increase LTV or decrease CAC (or both simultaneously).

Increasing LTV

  • Reduce churn through proactive customer success, better onboarding, and usage-based health scores
  • Expand revenue through upsells, cross-sells, and feature upgrades
  • Improve gross margins by optimising your cost of goods sold (COGS)

Decreasing CAC

  • Invest in SEO and content marketing to acquire organic traffic at low marginal cost
  • Build a referral programme that turns customers into acquisition channels
  • Improve landing page conversion rates to reduce cost-per-lead

Key Takeaways

  • LTV:CAC is the single best indicator of long-term business health.
  • Target a 3:1 ratio; anything below 1:1 is a crisis signal.
  • Always include all sales and marketing costs in your CAC calculation.
  • Reducing churn has the most powerful effect on LTV — prioritise retention.
  • A high ratio (>5:1) is a signal to invest more aggressively in growth.
